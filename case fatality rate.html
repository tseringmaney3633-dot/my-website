Strain-Specific Mortality

The genetic lineage of the virus dramatically affects clinical outcomes.

🇲🇾 NiV-M
Malaysia Lineage
40-50%
  • Regions: Malaysia, Singapore, Cambodia, Thailand
  • Malaysia Historical: ~39.6%
  • Key Feature: Less respiratory involvement
  • Transmission: Pig-to-human amplification
🇧🇩 NiV-B
Bangladesh Lineage
~75%
  • Regions: Bangladesh, India
  • Key Feature: Severe respiratory distress (ARDS)
  • Transmission: Direct bat-to-human via date palm sap
  • Compounding: Limited ICU access in rural areas
⚠️ Contributing Factors to NiV-B Lethality

The high mortality of NiV-B is closely linked to its propensity to cause severe respiratory distress (atypical pneumonia, ARDS). This is frequently compounded by a severe scarcity of high-level supportive care in the less-resourced rural areas where NiV-B outbreaks typically occur.

Clinical Risk Factors

Physiological and demographic markers correlated with high mortality.

👤
Age
Increasing age, particularly over 45 years, is a significant independent risk factor.
🧠
Neurological Signs
Seizures, segmental myoclonus, areflexia, and brainstem involvement are highly predictive of poor prognosis.
🧪
Lab Abnormalities
Elevated transaminases and severe thrombocytopenia significantly increase mortality risk.
🫁
Respiratory Failure
Rapid progression to ARDS is heavily tied to fatal outcomes.
🔬 2018 Kerala Data

19 out of 23 patients developed ARDS alongside acute encephalitis, and 18 of those 19 individuals died.

⚠️ Trade-off

Although survival rate is much higher for late-onset presentations, survivors face a drastically increased risk of permanent, severe residual neurological deficits (61%), including chronic fatigue, ataxia, myoclonus, and cranial nerve palsies.
