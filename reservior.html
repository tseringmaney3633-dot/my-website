Understanding Nipah Virus Epidemiology

Natural Reservoirs and Host Range

  • Natural Reservoirs: Fruit bats of the Pteropus genus (commonly referred to as flying foxes) are the established natural reservoirs of the Nipah virus (NiV). Specific species identified as reservoirs include Pteropus giganteus (frequently mapped in India and Bangladesh), Pteropus vampyrus, Pteropus hypomelanus, and Pteropus lylei. The virus has co-evolved with these bats over a long-standing period, allowing them to carry and shed the virus in their secretions (such as saliva, urine, feces, and seminal fluid) without exhibiting clinical signs of disease or significant pathological changes.
  • Host Range: Nipah virus exhibits an exceptionally broad mammalian host range, spanning across six different mammalian orders. Susceptible species include:
  1. Humans
  2. Pigs, which act as highly effective intermediate and amplifying hosts (particularly in the Malaysian and Singaporean outbreaks)
  3. Horses, which served as intermediate amplifying hosts during the 2014 Philippines outbreak
  4. Other domestic and laboratory animals, including dogs, cats, goats, cattle (cows), ferrets, and hamsters
  5. Notably, experimental infections cause severe disease in many of these hosts, whereas clinical signs are apparently absent in mice and rats. This wide host range is attributed to the highly conserved nature of the host cell receptors, Ephrin-B2 and Ephrin-B3, which the virus uses for cellular entry.
