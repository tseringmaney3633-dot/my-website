🦠 Nipah Virus (NiV)

An emerging, highly pathogenic zoonotic virus causing severe neurological and respiratory diseases in humans and animals. Classified as a Biosafety Level 4 (BSL-4) agent.

📋 Overview and Classification

Nipah virus (NiV) is an emerging, highly pathogenic zoonotic virus that causes severe and often fatal neurological and respiratory diseases in both humans and animals. Due to its high virulence, extreme lethality, and the historical lack of widely approved vaccines or treatments for human use, it is classified globally as a Biosafety Level 4 (BSL-4) agent.

NiV belongs to the genus Henipavirus within the subfamily Paramyxovirinae, family Paramyxoviridae (order Mononegavirales). The genus Henipavirus also contains Hendra virus (HeV), Cedar virus, Mojiang virus, and Ghana bat virus.

The virus was first identified in March 1999 during an outbreak among pig farmers in Kampung Sungai Nipah (Nipah River Village) in Malaysia, from which it derives its name.

Key Fact: NiV is an enveloped, pleomorphic, single-stranded, negative-sense, non-segmented RNA virus with a genome of approximately 18.2 kilobases.

🧬 Genomic Structure and Protein Composition

The Nipah virus genome contains six consecutively organized structural genes arranged in the order of 3'-N-P-M-F-G-L-5', which encode the virus's primary structural proteins:

Nucleocapsid Protein (N)

Binds directly to the viral RNA to form the viral ribonucleoprotein (vRNP) core. Responsible for governing viral RNA production and regulating viral polymerase activity.

Phosphoprotein (P)

Forms a crucial part of the vRNP complex alongside the N and L proteins. The P gene also serves as the template for multiple non-structural accessory proteins.

Matrix Protein (M)

Shapes the inner viral shell beneath the envelope. Coordinates viral assembly, morphogenesis, and the budding of new virions from host cells.

Fusion Glycoprotein (F)

Inserted into the outer viral membrane. Drives the fusion of the viral envelope with the host cell membrane, creating multinucleated giant cells (syncytia).

Attachment Glycoprotein (G)

Recognizes and binds to specific host cell receptors to initiate infection. Antibodies against the G protein are key to neutralizing viral infectivity.

Long Polymerase (L)

The large viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase enzyme responsible for synthesizing viral mRNA and replicating the negative-sense RNA genome.

Accessory and Non-Structural Proteins

  • V and W Proteins: Act as master immune-evasion factors, preventing the host cell from producing or responding to type I interferons by binding to cellular signaling proteins including STAT1, STAT2, RIG-I, and MDA5.
  • C Protein: Resides in the host cell cytoplasm, regulates viral RNA synthesis, mediates virion release, and acts as a key factor in overall viral virulence.

🔄 Transmission Dynamics

Nipah virus is transmitted via zoonotic spillover (animal-to-human) and direct human-to-human propagation, with dynamics heavily influenced by regional practices and viral lineages.

1. Interspecies Transmission

  • Pig-to-Human (Malaysia/Singapore): Pigs acted as highly efficient intermediate and amplifying hosts. Fruit bats contaminated pigsties, pigs developed severe respiratory symptoms ("barking pig syndrome"), and transmitted to farm workers.
  • Bat-to-Human (Bangladesh/India): Bypasses intermediate hosts. Spillover occurs through consumption of raw date palm sap contaminated by bats.
  • Horse-to-Human (Philippines): Implicated horses as amplifying hosts in 2014 through direct contact or consumption of undercooked horse meat.

2. Human-to-Human Transmission

Dominant in Bangladesh and India, accounting for ~50% of reported cases. Routes include:

  • Close physical contact and respiratory droplets
  • Super-spreaders: critically ill patients with severe respiratory involvement
  • Nosocomial transmission in healthcare settings without adequate PPE
  • Corpse-to-human transmission during traditional funeral practices

🔬 Pathogenesis and Pathology

The virus enters via the oro-nasal route, replicating in respiratory epithelial cells and lymphoid tissues. It enters the bloodstream, hitching a ride on host leukocytes without productive infection.

Cell Tropism: The virus targets cells expressing Ephrin-B2 (arterial endothelial cells, bronchial epithelial cells) and Ephrin-B3 (central nervous system neurons and glial cells).

Histopathology

  • Formation of multinucleated giant syncytial cells
  • Widespread endothelial destruction leading to systemic small-vessel vasculitis
  • Fibrinoid necrosis, thrombosis, ischemia, and microinfarctions
  • Pulmonary pathology: alveolar hemorrhage, pulmonary edema, ARDS

CNS Invasion

The virus invades the central nervous system through two pathways: hematogenously (disrupting the blood-brain barrier) or anterogradely from the nasal cavity via the olfactory nerves through the cribriform plate into the olfactory bulb.

🏥 Clinical Manifestations

The incubation period generally spans 3-14 days (mean of 8 days for NiV-M and 9.5 days for NiV-B).

Prodromal Phase

High-grade fever, severe headache, myalgia, sore throat, vomiting, lethargy, and extreme weakness.

Encephalitic Syndrome

Altered mental status, confusion, seizures, segmental myoclonus, cerebellar ataxia, and coma.

Respiratory Syndrome

Prominent in NiV-B lineage (70-83% of cases): severe cough, dyspnea, rapid progression to ARDS.

Case Fatality Rate: Ranges from 40% to 100%. The NiV-B strain is associated with a significantly higher CFR (~75%) compared to NiV-M (40-50%).

Long-Term Sequelae

  • Permanent Deficits: ~20-22% of survivors experience long-term neurological deficits.
  • Neuropsychiatric: Depression, violent outbursts, nightmares, and bedwetting.
  • Relapsed Encephalitis: Can occur months or years later (one case 11 years post-infection).

💊 Therapeutic Countermeasures

Currently, there are no FDA-approved therapeutics or antiviral drugs licensed specifically for human NiV infection; clinical management is primarily supportive.

Supportive Care

Maintaining fluid balance, managing intracranial pressure, treating seizures, and mechanical ventilation for ARDS.

Ribavirin

Broad-spectrum guanosine analog. Open-label trial showed 36% relative reduction in mortality, but efficacy remains debated.

Remdesivir

Adenosine analog. Pre-clinical studies show 100% protection in African green monkeys when initiated 24h post-infection.

Favipiravir

Selective RNA polymerase inhibitor. 100% protection in golden hamsters when administered daily for two weeks.

m102.4 Antibody

Humanized monoclonal antibody targeting G glycoprotein. Protected non-human primates up to 3 days post-exposure.

Fusion-Inhibitory Peptides

Cholesterol-tagged peptides block host-cell entry. Showed survival benefit in animal models.

🛡️ Prevention and Surveillance

Date Palm Sap Intervention

Educational campaigns discourage raw sap consumption. Installing physical barriers like bamboo skirts ("banas") completely blocks bats from accessing the sap stream.

💡 Solution: Boiling raw sap or fermenting it into molasses effectively inactivates the virus.

Hospital Infection Control

  • Place patients in airborne isolation rooms (negative-pressure)
  • Healthcare workers must wear N95 respirators, gloves, gowns, and eye protection
  • Disinfect environmental surfaces with agents approved for enveloped viruses

Surveillance Systems

Robust event-based, media-based, and sentinel surveillance systems are essential. "Clusters" of encephalitis (2+ cases within 21 days) trigger rapid investigation.

"One Health" Approach

Prevention requires coordinated strategy integrating medical, veterinary, and ecological systems to monitor bat-interface dynamics, land use, animal health, and human health.

Vaccine Candidates (in development)

  • Hendra G Subunit (HeV-sG): Registered for equine use in Australia; human Phase I trial underway
  • VSV-NiV-G: Live-attenuated vector vaccine; 100% protection in non-human primates
  • mRNA-1215: mRNA vaccine encoding prefusion F protein; Phase I clinical trial
  • Virus-Like Particles (VLPs): Contain G, F, M proteins; full protection in golden hamsters
