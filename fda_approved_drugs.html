What is Nipah virus?

Nipah virus (NiV) is a zoonotic paramyxovirus first identified during an outbreak among pig farmers in Malaysia in 1998. Its natural reservoir is fruit bats of the genus Pteropus, from which the virus can spill over into intermediate hosts or directly into humans.

Infection causes a spectrum of disease ranging from asymptomatic illness to severe encephalitis and acute respiratory distress, with recurring outbreaks reported in Bangladesh and India. No antiviral therapy or vaccine has yet received regulatory approval for human use.

Because Nipah virus is classified as a priority pathogen with epidemic potential, therapeutic development strategies that can move faster than de novo drug discovery are of particular research interest.

Strategy

Why drug repurposing?

Developing a novel antiviral from scratch typically takes over a decade and requires extensive safety testing before it ever reaches a patient. For a sporadic, outbreak-driven pathogen like Nipah virus, that timeline — and the commercial incentive behind it — rarely aligns with public health urgency.

Drug repurposing screens medicines that are already FDA-approved, with known human safety, dosing, and pharmacokinetic profiles, against Nipah viral protein targets. A hit identified this way can, in principle, move toward clinical evaluation far faster than a new chemical entity.

Novel drug discovery10–15y

Target validation → lead discovery → preclinical → three phases of clinical trials → approval.

FDA drug repurposing3–5y

Existing safety data lets candidates potentially skip directly to efficacy-focused trials.

Molecular Targets

Featured Nipah virus proteins

Six structural and non-structural proteins encoded by the Nipah genome, each explored here as an independent drug-target page.

L
Large protein — RNA-dependent RNA polymerase
Catalyzes viral genome replication and transcription.
M
Matrix protein
Drives viral assembly, structural integrity, and budding.
G
Attachment glycoprotein
Binds host ephrin-B2/B3 receptors to initiate infection.
F
Fusion glycoprotein
Mediates membrane fusion and viral / cell-cell entry.
N
Nucleoprotein
Encapsidates genomic RNA into the ribonucleoprotein complex.
P
Phosphoprotein
Essential polymerase cofactor; also encodes C, V and W proteins.
Studying targets in combination
L, M, G, F, N and P are also examined together as a combined target set — cross-referencing candidate drugs that show predicted or observed activity against more than one Nipah protein simultaneously.

Methodology

Research workflow

The five-stage pipeline this portal follows, from target selection through experimental validation.

01
Target identification
Selecting Nipah proteins — L, N, P, M, G, Combined or supporting proteins — as druggable targets.
02
Virtual screening
Screening the FDA-approved drug library against each target structure.
03
Molecular docking
Predicting binding pose, affinity, and interaction stability.
04
ADMET profiling
Evaluating absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity.
05
Wet-lab validation
Confirming top candidates experimentally in laboratory assays.
